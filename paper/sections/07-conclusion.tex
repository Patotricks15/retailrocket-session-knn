\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Retailrocket's browsing log has no durable user to personalise against, and
most sessions are too short for a sequence model to find much to condition
on. Session-based $k$-nearest-neighbours fits that regime directly: it
represents the current session as a set of items, retrieves similar past
sessions from a strictly earlier, leakage-safe pool, and lets a
similarity- and recency-weighted vote (Equations~\ref{eq:cosine}--\ref{eq:scoring})
produce the ranking -- no embeddings, no gradient descent, no sequence model
anywhere in the pipeline.

On the held-out test partition, tuned session-kNN modestly outperforms a
tuned popularity baseline on Recall@20 and MRR@20 while covering more than
thirteen times as much of the catalogue, and we have been explicit about the
one regime -- single-item ``cold'' sessions -- where popularity remains
competitive rather than presenting an unconditionally favourable picture.
Every point-in-time contract is enforced by a failing-by-default test, the
split is session-level and strictly temporal, and the serving path is
asserted to reproduce the offline ranking exactly rather than assumed to.

\subsection{Limitations and future work}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Synthetic fixture.} The headline numbers in
        Section~\ref{sec:results} are measured on the synthetic fixture, not
        the real Kaggle dataset, so the repository is runnable with no
        Kaggle account. \texttt{scripts/download\_data.py --kaggle}
        downloads the real log for anyone who wants to reproduce the result
        against it; we expect the direction of the comparison to hold but
        not necessarily the exact margins.
  \item \textbf{Item side-information is underused.} \texttt{item\_category}
        is engineered and tested but not yet blended into the scoring
        function; a category-aware fallback could plausibly close some of
        the cold-session gap reported in Section~\ref{sec:results}.
  \item \textbf{A single blended serving policy.} The current serving path
        (Section~\ref{sec:serving}) only falls back to popularity for
        zero-item requests. Extending the blend to short-but-nonzero
        sessions, as motivated in Section~\ref{sec:results}, is future work
        rather than a limitation of the method itself.
  \item \textbf{Candidate truncation.} \texttt{sample\_size} bounds the
        neighbour search for scalability; on a catalogue many times larger
        than this fixture's, a cheaper approximate retrieval step (e.g.
        locality-sensitive hashing over session vectors) would likely be
        needed to keep the per-request cost bounded without raising the
        truncation threshold.
\end{itemize}
